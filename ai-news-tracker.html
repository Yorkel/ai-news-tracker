Difference-in-Differences on a Censored Rating Scale Can Manufacture an Effect: Evidence from a Pre-Registered LLM-Judge Audit

evaluationmeasurementalignment

A pre-registered audit shows that difference-in-differences estimates run on censored rating scales can produce spurious interaction effects in LLM-judge comparisons. The authors demonstrate the artefact analytically, then reproduce it on live judge outputs where the true difference is zero.

Why this matters

Directly adjacent to the judge specification audit: a concrete mechanism by which analytic choices, not model behaviour, generate the headline effect. Candidate citation for the sensitivity framing.

Are Algorithm Registers Transparent? Perspectives from Germany

registerstransparencypublic sector

An empirical assessment of German public-sector algorithm registers against a transparency rubric. Entries are found to be inconsistently maintained, thin on evaluation detail, and rarely linked to the decisions the systems inform.

Why this matters

Registers are the closest existing instrument to the shared evidentiary substrate argument. Evidence that they fail on exactly the defend-and-challenge dimensions strengthens the landscape paper.

Whose Judgments Count? Representation Gaps in Crowdsourced Content Moderation Produce Unequal Protection from Perceived Toxicity

Analyses the demographic composition of crowdsourced moderation panels and shows that under-represented rater groups receive systematically weaker protection: content they rate as toxic is more likely to survive when panel majorities disagree.

Why this matters

Who bears the consequences of a classification is decided upstream, in who gets to label. Clean empirical hook for the voice and silence argument.

Accurate in Space, Unreliable in Time: How LLMs Represent National Cultural Change

cultural valuesrepresentationevaluation

Evaluates LLM representations of national cultural values against longitudinal survey data. Models capture cross-country differences well but freeze each country at roughly its 2015 values, missing documented within-country change.

Why this matters

Temporal validity as a distinct failure mode from cross-sectional accuracy. Speaks to the survey-equivalence instincts from the Ethiopia work, and to any claim built on model-derived country profiles.
